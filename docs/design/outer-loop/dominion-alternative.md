# Four-tree alternative — Will, Echoes, Memory, Dominion

**Status:** Fully recorded **alternative**, not the selected product structure. [Three-tree Will / Echoes / Anchors](upgrade-trees.md) remains the working recommendation. Dominion is **not** the old Currency multiplier tree with a new name; it represents durable *economic and material control*.

## 1. Why a fourth tree may be worth it

The strength of the three-tree scheme is its simplicity. Its main weakness is that **Anchors** must simultaneously contain remembering knowledge, persisting wealth, and building infrastructure. These activities may eventually be large enough that a single parent becomes a "miscellaneous permanence" drawer rather than a distinctive play style.

A four-tree system would give each of those mechanics a focused identity:

| Tree | Core fantasy | Question to player | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Will** | Bend an active moment | "What can I change now?" | Active intervention and controlled rewind |
| **Echoes** | Delegate mastered work | "What no longer needs me?" | Goal-driven routines and distinct selves |
| **Memory** | Carry learning and mastery across resets | "What have I learned that cannot be taken?" | Knowledge, training, limited discoveries and retained progress |
| **Dominion** | Own and invest value across resets | "What can I build and control that survives?" | Gold, materials, agreements, facilities and intertimeline economic leverage |

**Memory is not a duplicate of Echoes.** Remembered progress changes the **starting state** of a new timeline. Automation changes **who executes the work** after the timeline starts. A saved queue template is not itself preserved world mastery.

**Dominion is not a synonym for Nexus currency.** Nexus advancement buys permanent abilities in any tree; Dominion upgrades how ordinary-game **gold, investments, resources and physical infrastructure** can accumulate, transfer or generate bounded value. It should not print permanent upgrade currency for passive, unattended waiting.

## 2. Proposed four-way first choice

A first completed timeline still gives everyone **~10× baseline replay speed automatically**. Recommend one initial capability with all four options accessible for future unlocks.

| Choice | First upgrade | Concrete early benefit | Player archetype |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Will** | **Pivot** | Replan a bounded set of **current-run** unexecuted actions without starting over | Active optimizer |
| **Echoes** | **First Apprentice** | Automate a previously mastered conditional preparation goal | Planner / delegator |
| **Memory** | **Anchored Memory** | Persist one whitelisted mastery/discovery into the next timeline | Knowledge-focused strategist |
| **Dominion** | **Timeless Treasury** | Preserve a small **capped** reserve of gold through selected **ordinary loop** restarts, or grant a bounded starting-capital policy | Economic optimizer |

**Important:** In the four-tree alternative, Dominion's starter **does not automatically grant unlimited intertimeline gold transfer**. Carrying capital through one ordinary restart and carrying capital through a *full timeline reset* are different abilities; the second may be later, substantially more expensive or capped separately. Gold can buy mana in the original game, so small reserves may have outsized strategic power.

The first Dominion starter should clearly outperform "Earn +10% gold" in *interesting decisions*, but not trivialize the original resource acquisition loop. Its exact starting cap, eligible currencies and reset boundary must be designed from real playtests.

## 3. Four-tree progression sketch

| Tier | Will | Echoes | Memory | Dominion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I — Awakening** | **Pivot**: intervention in current loop | **First Apprentice**: one goal-based routine | **Anchored Memory**: one retained mastery | **Timeless Treasury**: capped retained gold / starting capital |
| **II — Mastery** | **Temporal Anchor**: controlled checkpoint | **Routine Chains**: conditions/fallbacks | **Remembered Path**: selected route knowledge, bounded zone unlock familiarity | **Starting Agreements**: supplies and bounded merchant arrangements |
| **III — Authority** | **Focused Reality**: limited outcome redirection | **Delegated Work**: specialist self assignments | **Inherited Discipline**: preserve part of an earned training accomplishment | **Compound Holdings**: finite persistent investments/facilities |
| **IV — Transcendence** | **Master of Moments**: commit one branched outcome | **Timeline Steward**: manage mastered timelines | **Living Archive**: curate a portfolio of retained memories | **Intertimeline Commerce**: bounded allocation across timelines |

This table is **not a linear skill list or a balancing commitment**. Each row is a conceptual power band; branches, rare synergies and alternate nodes could exist. Nothing is implemented.

### Tree-specific depth

**Memory** can include *Discoveries* (known routes/locations), *Training* (selected progress/skills) and *Patterns* (understanding recurring conditions). Retention should not overwrite story flags improperly or bypass the Explorer Guild survey bootstrap. The aim is to remove old knowledge friction while preserving discovery of new content.

**Dominion** can include *Treasury* (gold/reserves), *Agreements* (merchants, supplies and market terms), and *Structures* (workshops, logistics, durable production assets). Its long arc moves from carrying a few coins through an ordinary restart to maintaining a bounded economic estate that funds future timeline strategies. Its system has to respect **per-loop**, **per-timeline**, and **outer-persistent** accounting separately; gold/mana exchanges are a particularly important balance boundary.

**Will** and **Echoes** retain the descriptions, limitations and active-play accessibility expectations in the [three-tree design](upgrade-trees.md).

## 4. Cross-tree synergies if there are four

| Pair | Candidate | Combination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Will + Echoes** | **Adaptive Intervention** | Echoes ask for input at high-leverage decisions; Will can act without excessive prompts |
| **Will + Memory** | **Fixed Point** | Use an anchored learned state for a reversible experiment, with only one branch committed |
| **Echoes + Memory** | **Remembered Routine** | An Echo recognizes a known mastered strategy in a new timeline and proposes the legal conditional steps |
| **Echoes + Dominion** | **Persistent Workshop** | A specialist operates a durable preparation facility with explicit costs and capped output |
| **Memory + Dominion** | **Legacy Estate** | A remembered economic milestone unlocks improved trade arrangements or safely inherited investment terms |
| **Will + Dominion** | **Emergency Reserve** | Spend a limited anchored resource to salvage an otherwise doomed current loop |

These are concepts, not all required pairwise nodes. Implementing every possible pairing would create UI bloat; prefer a few unusually distinctive synergies.

### Beyond two-tree nodes

Three-way and four-way convergence should only appear if there is room for a late-game metaprogression system. The long-term vision remains coordinating multiple selves/timelines; **do not** immediately add six synergy branches, ten currencies or parallel workers.

## 5. Tradeoff analysis

| Criterion | Three: Will / Echoes / Anchors | Four: Will / Echoes / Memory / Dominion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-visit clarity | **Strong**: three memorable promises | Four interesting choices, but longer explanation |
| Currency gameplay depth | Anchors/Treasury may feel less visible | **Strong**: economic and infrastructure power gets equal prominence |
| Branch identity | Clean initially, but Anchors risks becoming broad | Four narrow identities; Memory needs enough unique *mechanical* content |
| Art/UI requirements | Three branches fit compact nexus scene | Four distinct motifs, navigation/facilities and onboarding beats |
| Long-term expansion | Good until persistence becomes huge | Better if markets, estates and cross-timeline trade become central |
| Upgrade parity/balance | Three substantially different opening powers | Harder: Gold→mana conversion could dominate other three |
| Data/migration | One fewer category/UX migration concern | More category IDs and migration UI; stable node IDs can mitigate |
| Character interplay | One self introduces three approaches | Four approaches can better match later specialist selves |

### Reasons to choose **three now**

- A small first nexus and a single alternate self should feel intimate, not like a complicated RPG talent screen.
- Currency, saved knowledge and infrastructure are all instances of a strong shared idea: **anchoring value against resets**.
- Early economic perks may have too little variety to support a full tree without padding it with +10% modifiers.
- The game's second run already gets **free ~10×**; first-choice clarity matters more than maximum branch count.

### Reasons to choose **four later** (or even before launch)

- Playtesting shows **Treasury/Structures** supports a rich independent upgrade path: persistent markets, crafting agreements, facilities and intertimeline resource transfers.
- Players consistently seek an **economic specialization** as distinct from remembered progress.
- Anchors has become two nearly unrelated progression systems, while Memory has enough independent nodes to be meaningful.
- A broader multi-self/timeline-management phase becomes the main game and benefits from independent social/knowledge/economic control dimensions.

**Current recommendation:** design content with stable **ability IDs** and independent categories so Dominion can eventually be promoted from an Anchors specialization without having to reset player progression. Launch with three *unless* design/playtests demonstrate four genuinely strong, distinct starting choices.

## 6. How to promote Anchors into Memory + Dominion safely

Do **not** implement three trees by making a single persisted integer `anchorsLevel` that implicitly grants Memory/Treasury/Structures bonuses. That makes a later split ambiguous and can duplicate or erase earned powers.

Instead, assign stable individual abilities (illustrative IDs, subject to naming approval):

```js
// CONCEPT ONLY — not a live schema or authorized API
ownedAbilities = [
  "will.pivot",
  "echoes.first_apprentice",
  "memory.anchored_memory",
  "economy.timeless_treasury",
  "structures.persistent_workshop"
];
```

The **display taxonomy** can map `memory.*`, `economy.*` and `structures.*` into a common **Anchors** branch when using three trees; a later four-tree presentation simply exposes `memory.*` under **Memory** and `economy.*`/`structures.*` under **Dominion**. A player's entitlements remain identical.

Requirements before implementation: no duplicated free starter grant, no silent refunds or purchase-price changes on taxonomy-only migration, tested eligibility for cross-tree synergies and explicit policy if a purchased "Anchors-wide" node would split. Ability-level prerequisites should not assume `treeIndex=2` is immutable.

## 7. What would make this decision evidence-based?

- **First-choice playtest:** Give players the same original completion and ~10× speed, then compare whether Anchored Memory and Timeless Treasury are both compelling—and whether they feel different.
- **Midgame audit:** Count genuinely different unlockable **decisions** in Memory and Dominion, not just numerical buffs. If each supports an independent meaningful progression arc, four deserves serious consideration.
- **Economic safety benchmark:** Simulate capped gold carryover, Buy Mana conversions, the original investment system and late-game accelerations. If an early Dominion pick obliterates resource planning, weaken or delay it before making it a full pillar.
- **Onboarding comprehension:** Players should be able to explain the four options after a short conversation with the first self.
- **Migration clarity:** Confirm ability IDs can survive changing branches, and the user sees why the same upgrade moved without losing it.

No upgrade costs, node counts, level caps, live UI mockups or release deadlines are approved by documenting this alternative. The next product decision to settle is [RW-Q13](decisions.md).
