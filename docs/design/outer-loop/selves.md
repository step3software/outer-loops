# Alternate selves — characters, progression and future delegation

**Status:** Meeting an alternate protagonist in Real World is **approved**. Named personalities, recruitment conditions, abilities and population rules remain **proposals**.

## Core fantasy

The player is not collecting arbitrary heroes. They are meeting **people who could have been them**. Alternate selves should have recognizable common traits, but different memories, priorities, relationships and skills based on how their timelines unfolded.

The source's `Restore Time` story describes alternate lives such as befriending the Witch and settling in Merchanton as a crafter. This supports recognizable selves without forcing a lore retcon.

## First self — an actual character, not a vendor

Design intent: one other self awaits at the first entrance. They are the initial guide to the nature of timelines, but their knowledge is partial. Their presence immediately demonstrates that restoring time created consequences beyond the nine-zone journey. They should be revisitable and have evolving dialogue.

**Do not** introduce a crowd of helpers or an upgrade-list popup before the player encounters this character. The first choice they offer should be about the player's next **timeline**, not a purchase-only transaction. Their exact name, origin, motivations and tutorial role require narrative approval.

## What makes an alternate self meaningful

- **Distinct identity:** a clear alternate life story, a recognizable voice and a different position on the central mystery.
- **Functional effect:** a capability that changes player decisions (e.g., a preparation plan) rather than a 5% bonus badge.
- **Progression:** limited upgrades, trust/memory or expanded responsibility as the nexus develops.
- **Constraints:** one self cannot do every task; assignment tradeoffs and conflicts create strategic choices.
- **Feedback:** player can see what a self prepared or discovered, and why an assignment succeeded or failed.
- **Narrative continuity:** later conversations refer to prior nexus decisions and completed timelines.

## Relation to the proposed Echoes progression tree

The [Echoes branch](upgrade-trees.md) is the *player's automation and delegation capability*; **alternate selves are persistent characters in the wider nexus**, not a consumable resource that exists only if the player buys Echoes. A player who chooses Will or Anchors first **still meets the first other self** and can talk to them.

A later Echoes node such as **First Apprentice** allows the player to give a familiar, bounded conditional objective to an eligible self; specialists may have different job options, costs and constraints. This is distinct from the already existing `Actions.next` queue and loadouts. The same selves can matter to Will, Memory or Treasury stories without acting as redundant passive bonus vendors.

In the [four-tree Dominion alternative](dominion-alternative.md), an Echo may eventually operate a durable workshop or economic arrangement, but **owning Dominion assets must not automatically simulate an extra full timeline**. Cross-tree **Persistent Workshop** is proposed, not approved.

## Timeline identity and recruitment rules

**Recommended:** many completed timeline **records**, relatively few **distinct recruited selves**. Distinctive outcomes/builds/challenge resolutions may create opportunities to meet a self; repeated identical clears should not spawn duplicate NPCs every time. A roster entry can be associated with an originating timeline, but recruitment criteria must be explicit and player-understandable.

Avoid unconstrained procedural character generation at first: it multiplies art/dialogue costs and makes each self feel interchangeable. Hand-authored initial selves can teach the concept and become templates for later systemic variants.

## Delegation model — long-term direction, not MVP requirement

Start with a single *visible*, finite delegation task performed by one established self. Only later add:
- bounded job slots and durations;
- conditional task selection with earned automation;
- synergy/conflict between selves;
- summarized work on other timelines;
- deeper coordination of multiple timelines.

Do not simulate every self as an independent full game instance or silently generate uncapped resources while the player is offline. Consider performance, persistence, deterministic replay and fairness before allowing multi-threaded timeline execution.

## Open questions and tests

Which self is first? Can selves return to their original lives? Are there irreversible outcomes? Does a self join because of a completed branch or because the nexus rescues them? How many can be active simultaneously? What is the minimum first-self contribution that feels meaningful *without* trivializing the original loop?

Playtest for recognition ("they are me"), attachment (player remembers individual selves), decision impact (assignments matter), and clutter (hub remains readable). These questions are captured as RW-Q07–Q10 in [decisions](decisions.md).
